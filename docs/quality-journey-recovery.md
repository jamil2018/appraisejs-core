# Quality Journey pause and recovery

Local Journey detail controls pause and resume through project-scoped Server Actions. They use expected lifecycle hash, operational version and an idempotency key. Pause changes operational status and records an immutable audit event; it preserves lifecycle hashes, accepted answers, approvals, consents and sealed evidence. Exact lost-reply replay recovers the existing receipt. Pause persists its browser-session and execution-cycle targets in the same transaction. Replayed or delayed cleanup uses that snapshot and its pause epoch, so it cannot cancel newly resumed work. A fresh resume recovers cleanup after a crash between pause commit and cleanup.

Pause prevents new commands, claims, handoffs, browser launches and execution admissions. It fences unaccepted external attempts. Accepted work and human waits remain available for recovery. An already admitted asynchronous spawn can reach the process boundary after pause; its post-spawn fence requests cleanup and resume remains blocked until reconciliation. Appraise does not stop external Codex tasks or prove offline/quota enforcement.

Managed cancellation first records `CANCELLING`. On supported POSIX hosts, a detached supervisor owns the group lifetime. The hub requests stop through private IPC; only the live supervisor signals its own group. Repeated requests share one outcome, including uncertain failure. The hub observes supervisor exit and absence of non-zombie group members; it never signals a persisted numeric group ID. Managed Cucumber hooks announce browser launch intent before launch and acknowledge closure only after awaiting launch and closing the browser. Terminal cancellation requires a hash-bound supervisor-v1 receipt with `CLOSE_ACKNOWLEDGED` and durable output completion. Either durable write reconciles their join, and a fresh client can recover after both writes; a late receipt has no polling deadline. A never-started attempt has a separate receipt. Unsupported hosts, inaccessible process inspection and lost ownership remain unresolved; a cancellation request is not a termination observation.

Discovery browser admission records a durable ownership row before launching. Cleanup fences the session before closing the runtime and stores a sanitized observation. A new hub refuses prior-process live, launching, pending or unknown ownership. Persisted PIDs are never authority to kill or reattach. A hub crash may therefore require explicit resource recovery before resume; absence from a process-local map does not prove cleanup.

Terminal execution bytes may be sealed while paused, but their lifecycle result is deferred until resume. Resume reconciles existing durable outcomes, checks browser cleanup and nonterminal/ambiguous execution, and never turns an uncertain launch into a fresh retry. Publishing deferred sealed results and reopening admissions share one transaction; publication failure rolls back resume. A forced process exit without a browser-close acknowledgement remains unresolved, even when the capsule group has exited. Lease expiry and takeover fence predecessor generations; observed task identity remains recovery metadata, not authority. The MCP contract does not expose these local human pause/resume controls as agent-owned lifecycle decisions.
